## Scope and applicability

This procedure applies to every model in the range when a customer reports
that an item expected with their delivery was not in the box: goggles, a mains
cord, a filter, a spare lamp or the instructions for use.

It is a customer-service and logistics procedure, not a device-fault
procedure. It covers establishing what is actually missing and raising the
right product movement. It does not cover any fault with the device itself.

## Symptom confirmation

Ask the customer to confirm which items they have received, not only which
they believe are missing. Packing varies and accessories are often in a
separate compartment under the moulded insert rather than loose in the carton.

Ask them to check that compartment before anything is shipped, and ask for the
part name as printed on the carton label where one is visible.

## Step 1 — Confirm the expected contents

Check the order record for what was actually on the shipment. A customer may
expect an item that was never ordered, which is a different conversation from
an item that was ordered and not sent.

Where the order record and the customer's account disagree, treat the order
record as what was promised and escalate the difference rather than deciding
it on the call.

## Step 2 — Decide the movement

A missing accessory is resolved by sending the accessory. The device itself
stays with the customer and no return is raised. Only the missing items move,
outbound.

Where the missing item cannot be identified confidently from the customer's
description, do not guess a part number; pass the case to a person.

## Step 3 — Disposition

Record which items were confirmed missing and raise the outbound movement for
approval. Do not promise a despatch date before the movement has been
approved.
